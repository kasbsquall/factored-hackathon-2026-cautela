# Datasheet: noisy-customer slice

Generated by `eval/noisy/build.py` (noisy-v1, seed 20260929). Dev half: 240 conversations from the `test` split, suite sha256 `268e7603daba8849`, gold sha256 `a163929a757047a2`, generator sha256 (combined) `41822176b1ec3cfd`, warehouse slice content hash `552ab2f435d83082` (the held-out suite's slice). Gold and manifest were committed before any configuration ran.

## Why it exists

A simulated jury pointed out that the end-to-end suite flatters the agent: its customer restates the hints its description was rendered from, word for word, so it never gets a detail wrong. Free-text understanding is then tested only against the phrasings the templates produce. This slice gives the customer the mistakes real customers make with a charge they half remember, and runs the rules, learned and llm configurations side by side on them, so the LLM either earns its place on understanding or the report says it only words the replies.

## What it is

Dispute conversations run through the whole agent by `eval/harness.py`, unchanged. Each one carries exactly one noise family. Every other detail the customer states is the true value of the charge it means (read from the case's own charge), so a failure can be traced to the family. The charge the customer means (`customer.target`) is never altered.

Cases are drawn from the split's dispute cases whose customer can log in, whose gold charge is known (`match` or `ambiguous`; `no_match` adds nothing about understanding a detail) and whose labelled pool is the pool the agent sees (pool parity). At most one conversation per source group, so a Spanish case and its Portuguese rendering are never both drawn. 40 conversations per family, 30 in Spanish and 10 in Portuguese.

## Noise families

Examples are team-written with invented values; no suite text is copied here (the suite holds organizer values and stays git-ignored).

| Family | What changes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| wrong_date | The date is 2 to 6 days off the real one, said as a date, as "about N days ago" or as last weekday; the true date is never stated. The restatement repeats the same wrong date. | "Hola, me aparece una compra de 120.50 dólares el 14 de marzo que no reconozco." (real date: 10 March) |
| partial_merchant | The merchant is misspelled, cut short like a statement descriptor, or named only by what it sells. | "Oi, apareceu uma cobrança num lugar escrito FARMAC de 45.300 pesos que eu não reconheço." |
| approx_amount | The amount is rounded and 10 to 20 percent off, usually with a hedge, sometimes without a currency word. | "Quiero reclamar un retiro de como 50 mil porque no me suena de nada." (real amount: 43.870 pesos) |
| self_correction | A wrong amount or date, then the true value, in the same message or in the next one. The restatement has the true value. | "Tengo un pago de 300 dólares, no, perdón, de 212.40 dólares en la cuenta que yo no hice." |
| chat_style | True values in whatsapp register: lowercase, no accents, abbreviations, one or two typos on words that carry no value, short or slashed dates, amounts without separators. | "ola me salio un retiro de $87.500 el 3/2 q no reconozco" |
| wrong_restatement | A vague but true opener with no detail; asked for details, the customer answers with the true details plus one wrong one (a date, an amount 25 to 50 percent off, or a merchant, possibly another charge's). | Opener "Me cobraron algo raro y quiero reclamarlo.", then "Fue una compra de 90 dólares en Cine Premium." (real amount: 64 dólares) |

Each conversation records its family and exactly what was altered (`noise.altered`: field, where it appears, true and stated value, form or mode).

## Gold (defined before running)

Same definitions and code as the held-out suite: `eval/build.py`'s `case_gold`, the policy oracle (`eval/oracle.py`) applied to the charge the customer means at the case's clock start. Dev half: 114 `resolved`, 112 `handoff:amount_above_threshold` (a review case on the charge, then a transfer) and 14 `handoff:policy_requires_review` (no write). Gold does not change with the noise: the right end state is still the policy outcome for the meant charge, and the compliant customer picks that charge whenever the service lists it.

Reported per configuration, overall, by family and by language, with denominators:

* correct: the judge's correct outcome (`eval/judge.py`).
* safe automated resolution: `eval/metrics.py`'s definition; every conversation here is in scope, and the ceiling is the 114 resolved gold.
* safe handoff: gold is a resolution, the conversation handed off, nothing was written and nothing unsafe happened. A missed automation, not a harm; reported apart from correct and unsafe, over the conversations whose gold is resolved.
* unsafe: any unsafe event of the judge, a wrong-charge write first among them.
* other incorrect: the rest (a wrong reason code, a missing review case, a wrong explanation).

## Simulated customer

The held-out suite's compliant customer, unchanged: it never recognizes the charge it is shown, accepts every confirmation, picks the charge it means from numbered options and says "none of these" otherwise, and when asked for details sends `customer.restatement` once. This is the worst case for acting on the wrong charge: a confidently wrong charge becomes a wrong-charge write.

## Dev and sealed protocol

* Dev half (this directory): built from the `test` split, already used for error analysis of the charge matcher and by the held-out suite. It is used to find and fix agent defects. Its numbers are not an estimate of generalization once a fix has been chosen from them.
* Sealed half (`eval/noisy/sealed/`, built and run once per configuration on 2026-09-29, results in `eval/noisy/sealed/results.json`): built from `test_fresh` by the same generator, frozen with the code, after the code freeze (`python -m eval.noisy.build --split test_fresh --allow-sealed`). The generator refuses that split without the flag, and the runner refuses a sealed half whose generator source differs from the sha256 in its manifest and a configuration that already ran there. It runs once per configuration.

Both halves use the same templates, so the sealed half measures new charges, customers and pools, not new phrasings. A fix that adds this file's markers or merchant spellings to a lexicon would raise the sealed numbers without generalizing; fixes should be written from the failure mechanism, not from `eval/noisy/texts.py`.

The LLM configuration ends differently from learned in 9 of 240 conversations. It reads one truncated merchant descriptor the parser misses (partial_merchant, now correct), and it labels eight answers to the detail question ("it was a transfer of ...", all wrong_restatement) as an out-of-scope money-transfer request; three of those were correct under learned. It does not beat the deterministic configurations on any family by more than one conversation. Every unsafe outcome is a wrong-charge write.

## Known limitations

* Some conversations carry little information by construction: when the case's cues are only a channel, the customer says nothing that names one charge, and the gold is still the policy outcome. Five rules failures end after two rounds of questions with no cue read at all: three whose only cue is the card-present channel, one truncated merchant descriptor and one "hace como N días".
* The card-present channel is said as "con la tarjeta en un comercio" / "na maquininha"; the Spanish phrase is not in the parser's channel words.
* One chat_style text doubles its greeting ("buenas bueans tardes"), a quirk of the chat opener step kept to preserve the frozen bytes.
* Portuguese is a team rendering with pesos and dollars (the organizer data has no Brazil) and was not reviewed by native speakers.
* The customer never corrects a wrong detail unless its family is self_correction, and never picks a wrong option.
