## Headline figures

* Why this workflow: disputes rank 7 of 7 candidate workflows by agent-hours on contacts not resolved first time (278.3 agent-hours per year against 2,648.7 for contact reason Complaint). They were chosen because each outcome can be verified against the customer's own transactions, which makes automating them checkable and safe to measure (section 1).
* Unrecognized-charge complaints ("Cargo no reconocido"): 12,297 of 67,095 complaints over 1,098 days, 18.3%, volume rank 1 of 10 complaint types, 103 complaints ahead of the next type (section 2).
* SLA breached: 20.4% (2,507 / 12,297). Resolution time p50 15.0 days, p90 27.0 days (n = 2,862). Reception: 50.4% by call center, 25.3% by App or Web (section 4).
* Cost per resolution: a human dispute intake costs USD 1.21 at a placeholder USD 10 per agent-hour (7.2 minutes); a safe automated resolution costs USD 0.000630 of LLM tokens, measured in `eval/results_after_fix.json` (hosting not measured).
* Savings range, central handling time: 50.9 agent-hours per year (USD 509 per year) with the deployed configuration's end-to-end rate of 40.8% on App and Web disputes only, up to 212.3 agent-hours per year (USD 2,123) with the component safe act rate of 56.9% and call-center disputes routed to the flow (an assumption). Failed attempts are charged at full human cost plus tokens. These are offline projections; no saving was measured in production (section 9).
* Handoff queue: at most 8.1 handoffs on a p95 day and 2.3 agent-minutes in the busiest weekday-hour, a fraction of one agent, so the existing complaint queue can absorb them (section 5).
* Policy thresholds over the whole window: USD 450 sends 45.5% (1,630,753 / 3,586,032) of disputable charges to a person, 83.7% of them transfers and payments; the fraud rule sends 0.098%, exactly the flagged charges (section 7).
* Satisfaction: CSAT averages 3.00 when a contact is resolved first time and 2.00 when it is not; surveys cannot be linked to disputes (section 8).

## 1. Why this workflow: candidate workflows ranked by agent-hours

The problem statement names four example workflows. The contact center records six coarse contact reasons, so each workflow is mapped to its closest reason (team judgment), and the dispute workflow is sized from its complaints with the Complaint reason's handling time and first-contact resolution as proxies.

* `agent_hours_per_year` = contacts_per_year * handling_seconds / 3600
* `unresolved_contact_hours_per_year` = agent_hours_per_year * (1 - first_contact_resolution)

All contact-center handling adds up to 20,373.3 agent-hours per year (686,296 interactions over 1,098 days). By this measure the dispute workflow is small: it ranks 7 of 7, with 278.3 agent-hours per year on contacts not resolved first time, against 2,648.7 for contact reason Complaint (9.5 times as many). Other workflows are larger by agent-hours.

Disputes were chosen for verifiability and for the safety of automating them. A dispute is the one candidate whose right outcome a record the bank holds settles: the charge is in the customer's own transactions, and the claim window, amount and fraud signals decide what may happen to it; 73.2% (8,998 / 12,297) of dispute complaints have at least one non-deposit transaction of the customer in the 90 days before the complaint, where the agent looks for it. Every automated outcome can therefore be checked against records, in the evaluation and in production. Opening a dispute is also a write that touches money, so the cost of an unchecked mistake is higher than for an informational answer; the team judged that automation should start where each action can be verified before it is taken.

By total agent-hours the largest candidate is contact reason Transactional (4,894.5 agent-hours per year). The partly verifiable workflows (account or payment inquiries and card-service support) would become candidates once the bank records what the customer asked and what was done, which this data does not.

## 7. Policy thresholds: what they send to a person

Both thresholds are read from `agent/policy/rules.yaml` (version 2026-09-26.2), and amounts are converted to USD the way the policy engine does (amount_usd, else the fixed rates of SYN-FX-001). Base: gold.dispute_policy_inputs, every transaction in the window with a disputable status that is not a deposit (statuses Approved, Pending; excluded types Deposit).

**SYN-AMOUNT-001, USD amount at or above USD 450.00.** 3,586,032 disputable charges over the window; USD amount p50 USD 414.10, p75 USD 1,510.78, p90 USD 5,786.86, p95 USD 7,892.47, p99 USD 9,575.13. The rule sends 45.5% (1,630,753 / 3,586,032) of them to a person.

|---|---|---|---|
| Purchase | 1,017,763 | 10.1% (102,432 / 1,017,763) | USD 450.32 |
| Withdrawal | 907,012 | 10.4% (94,592 / 907,012) | USD 452.05 |
| Transfer | 842,517 | 96.5% (812,707 / 842,517) | USD 9,003.32 |
| Payment | 694,638 | 79.4% (551,884 / 694,638) | USD 1,806.00 |
| Adjustment | 124,102 | 55.7% (69,138 / 124,102) | USD 901.54 |

The p90 rationale holds for purchases and withdrawals (10.1% and 10.4% at or above the threshold). Transfer, Payment and Adjustment amounts are larger: 96.5%, 79.4% and 55.7% of them reach the threshold, and they carry most of the share the rule sends to a person. How much of this applies to disputed charges depends on which types customers dispute, which the data does not record (no complaint names a transaction); the evaluation's case mix is a team choice (`ml/DATASHEET.md`).

Reproducing the one-month analysis the rules.yaml comment quotes: in 2024-03 the warehouse holds 121,588 transactions, 29,739 of them purchases, 12,611 with amount_usd, whose p90 is USD 452.60. Over the 37 months of the window the monthly purchase p90 ranges from USD 447.90 to USD 452.97, so the month chosen was typical and the threshold sits near the purchase p90 of any month.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| False | 4,420,692 | 3,536,426 | 30.0 | 15.0 | 0 |

The largest score of an unflagged transaction is 30.0; 2,373 of 3,425 flagged transactions with a score sit above it. Among disputable charges the rule routes 0.098% (3,502 / 3,586,032) to a person, 0 of them by the score without the flag. In this data the fraud rule sends exactly the flagged charges, and any score threshold above the unflagged maximum would route the same charges. The value 50 keeps a margin above that maximum for data where score and flag disagree; it is a team choice with no outcome data behind it, and it does not change the workload here. In 2024-03: 135 flagged transactions, 53 of them at or above 50; unflagged maximum 30.0. The counts in the rules.yaml comment come from the warehouse as it was when the comment was written.

Either threshold together sends 45.5% (1,632,719 / 3,586,032) of disputable charges to a person (0 charges lack a USD amount after conversion). The claim-window rule depends on the day of the conversation and is not counted here.

## 8. Customer satisfaction

Surveys link to contact-center interactions, never to complaints: 100.0% (212,759 / 212,759) of surveys carry an interaction id found in the contact center, and complaints carry no interaction id (section 10), so no survey can be tied to a dispute.

CSAT surveys (127,856, scores 1 to 4 as observed) average 3.00 when the contact was resolved first time (n = 97,851) and 2.00 when it was not (n = 30,005). The gap is between 0.96 and 1.01 points in every contact reason, and mean CSAT by reason follows its first-contact resolution: Complaint 2.43 (FCR 43.4%), Retención 2.61 (FCR 60.4%), Commercial 2.66 (FCR 65.3%), Technical 2.69 (FCR 69.4%), Product 2.90 (FCR 89.7%), Transactional 2.91 (FCR 91.5%). In this data satisfaction moves with first-contact resolution, which makes it the customer outcome to target: a dispute taken end to end in one conversation is what the surveys reward. The dispute workflow's own effect on CSAT cannot be measured, since disputes cannot be linked to a contact.

The complaint-level score (`resolution_satisfaction`) exists for 3.8% (471 / 12,297) of disputes; across all types it is present only on complaints with status Closed (2,484 complaints). Its values (1 to 5) are spread almost evenly (1: 87, 2: 91, 3: 96, 4: 98, 5: 99); the mean is 3.07 for disputes and 3.01 for other complaints, and across all complaints it does not follow resolution time: 0-7 days 3.06 (n = 541), 8-15 days 3.05 (n = 657), 16-30 days 2.97 (n = 1,176). It cannot rank workflows or measure a dispute outcome.

## 10. Where the data is uniform or templated, and what that means

* Complaint types with a subcategory are near uniform: 5 types between 11,886 and 12,297 complaints (max/min 1.035, coefficient of variation 0.0136).
* SLA breach rate per complaint type stays between 18.1% and 20.6%.
* `sla_breached` does not follow resolution time: median 15.0 days when breached (n = 3,058) and 16.0 days when not (n = 12,305).
* Interactions by hour of day: max/min 1.029 across 24 hours (coefficient of variation 0.0062). Only the weekday pattern varies.
* The repeat-complainer flag agrees with the history in 333 of 10,086 flagged complaints; 2,315 of 67,095 complaints have an earlier one within 90 days.
* Unrecognized-charge complaints carry 1 distinct description text(s) over 12,297 rows.
* Call transcripts: 546 distinct texts over 171,321 rows, 42 distinct customer turns, 1 distinct detected intent value(s).
* Complaints linked to an interaction: 0.0% (0 / 67,095). No complaint names a transaction.
* Fraud label on transactions: 0.098% (4,316 / 4,425,008). Mexican customers' transactions in USD: 100.0% (2,216,431 / 2,216,431).
* Customers whose registration branch is missing from branches: 99.997% (149,995 / 150,000).
* Claimed-amount currency does not follow the customer's country (complaints per pair): Argentina ARS 210, Argentina COP 182, Argentina MXN 212, Argentina USD 219, Colombia ARS 306, Colombia COP 303, Colombia MXN 297, Colombia USD 316, Mexico ARS 490, Mexico COP 527, Mexico MXN 525, Mexico USD 489.

What this means for the conclusions. Where the outcome metrics (SLA breach, escalation, resolution time, repeat contact, satisfaction) sit within a few tenths of a point across complaint types, countries and segments, they cannot rank workflows. By workload the dispute workflow ranks 7 of 7 candidates (section 1), and by complaint volume it ties the next type (section 2). The choice of unrecognized charges rests on verifiability: the facts of a dispute can be checked against records the bank holds (the customer's own transactions, with merchant, channel, amount and date), which is what the agent's tools verify before any write, and what lets the evaluation score every automated outcome. The text fields are templated, so language understanding is evaluated on team-generated scenarios (`ml/reports/results.md`, `eval/report.md`), and the savings above are projections that assume the offline rates carry over to real traffic.
